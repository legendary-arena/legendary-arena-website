A rigid deck performs well in its ideal matchup and poorly in every other one. A flexible deck performs well across a range of matchups because it adapts. The tool that enables adaptation without sacrificing identity is the flex slot — a designated position in your deck that changes based on the game in front of you.

## What is a flex slot

A flex slot is not a weak slot. It is an intentionally open position where you recruit the card that best addresses the current game's demands. Your core cards — the engine, the synergy pieces, the primary payload — stay the same game to game. The flex slots adjust.

Think of your deck in two tiers:

**Core (70–80% of your deck).** These cards define your strategy. They are recruited consistently across all games. They are your engine, your synergy backbone, your primary damage output.

**Flex (20–30% of your deck).** These positions are filled during the game based on the matchup. Against aggressive Scenarios, flex slots become defensive cards. Against slow Scenarios, flex slots become additional scaling.

## Designing flex slots

### Step 1: Build your core first

Identify the cards your deck absolutely needs to function. The draw engine, the key synergy pieces, the finisher. These are non-negotiable. Recruit them with priority and do not compromise on them for matchup-specific cards.

### Step 2: Designate flex positions

After your core is built (usually by mid-game), your remaining recruits are flex slots. You have two to four recruits remaining. Instead of defaulting to "what is the best card available," ask: "what does this game need from me that my core does not provide?"

### Step 3: Fill flex slots based on the game

**Need more defense?** Recruit a card that stabilizes or controls threats.
**Need more speed?** Recruit a cheap, efficient attacker that accelerates your closing window.
**Need more utility?** Recruit a thinning or filtering effect that tightens your deck for the final turns.

The key insight: flex slots are not second-choice recruits. They are targeted solutions to the specific game you are playing.

## Flex vs unfocused

An unfocused deck buys random cards with no plan. A flex-slot deck has a clear core and uses remaining positions strategically. The difference is intentionality.

Signs your deck is unfocused:
- You cannot identify which cards are core and which are flex
- More than a third of your deck was recruited "because it looked good"
- Your deck does not have a clear strategy sentence — it just has cards

Signs your deck is using flex slots well:
- You can explain why each flex recruit was chosen for this specific game
- Removing the flex cards would leave a coherent core deck behind
- Your flex recruits address a gap your core does not cover

## Flex slots in tournament play

In tournaments, flex-slot thinking separates good players from great ones. Two players with the same core strategy can have different results purely based on how well they adapt their flex slots to each round's Scenario.

Between rounds, review: what did this game demand that my core did not provide? Was my flex slot effective, or did I default to a generic recruit? Each round is a learning opportunity for the next one.

## Try this

In your next game, after recruiting your core engine and synergy pieces, consciously designate your remaining two to three recruits as flex slots. Before each flex recruit, state out loud what problem it solves for this specific game. After the game, evaluate whether those flex choices contributed or whether generic recruits would have been equally good.
