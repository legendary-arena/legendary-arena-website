Thirteen weeks of fundamentals. Deck construction, resource curves, Scenario scouting, turn sequencing, hand evaluation, threat assessment, early-game priorities, mid-game pivots, endgame closing, trap avoidance, and co-op coordination. Each skill was presented individually. Now they combine.

The Foundations Trial is not a test you pass or fail. It is a benchmark — a structured way to measure where your fundamentals stand and where they need work. Five scenarios, each emphasizing a different skill cluster. Score yourself honestly. The number at the end tells you what to practice next.

## How the benchmark works

Play five games. Each game targets a specific skill cluster from Q1. After each game, score yourself on three criteria using a 1–3 scale:

- **1 — Struggled.** The skill felt uncertain; decisions were reactive or inconsistent.
- **2 — Competent.** You applied the skill intentionally; some mistakes but mostly solid.
- **3 — Confident.** The skill felt automatic; you made decisions quickly and correctly.

Maximum score per scenario: 9 (three criteria at 3 each). Maximum total: 45.

## Scenario 1: The Construction Test

**Focus:** Deck construction, resource curve, strategy sentence.

**Setup:** Choose a Scenario with a broad recruitment row and moderate difficulty. Before drawing your first hand, write your strategy sentence and sketch your target resource curve on a piece of paper.

**Score criteria:**
- Did your finished deck match your strategy sentence? (1–3)
- Was your resource curve balanced and intentional? (1–3)
- Did you successfully apply the [keep/buy/skip framework](/posts/week-06-hand-evaluation/) for every recruit? (1–3)

## Scenario 2: The Sequencing Test

**Focus:** Turn sequencing, play order, information-before-commitment.

**Setup:** Choose a Scenario that offers draw effects and conditional triggers. For every turn, physically sort your hand left to right: draw effects, synergy triggers, attack/recruit.

**Score criteria:**
- Did you play draw effects before making commitments every turn? (1–3)
- Did you identify and activate synergy triggers in the correct order? (1–3)
- Did proper sequencing change your play at least three times during the game? (1–3)

## Scenario 3: The Threat Assessment Test

**Focus:** The danger ladder, attack allocation, threat prioritization.

**Setup:** Choose an aggressive Scenario with multiple simultaneous threats. Before committing attack each turn, verbally rank every threat on the board as Tier 1, 2, or 3.

**Score criteria:**
- Did you correctly identify Tier 1 threats before they caused irreversible damage? (1–3)
- Did you address Tier 2 scaling threats before they became emergencies? (1–3)
- Did you avoid wasting attack on Tier 3 passive threats when higher-priority targets existed? (1–3)

## Scenario 4: The Adaptation Test

**Focus:** Early-game priorities, mid-game pivoting, reading the board.

**Setup:** Choose a Scenario that starts in one direction and shifts mid-game (escalation change, new threat types, altered win conditions). Plan your first three buys before the game starts.

**Score criteria:**
- Were your first three buys aligned with the Scenario's opening demands? (1–3)
- Did you recognize the pivot point and adjust your strategy within two turns of the shift? (1–3)
- Did your deck after the pivot still function as a coherent engine, or did it feel patched together? (1–3)

## Scenario 5: The Closing Test

**Focus:** Endgame closing, the close plan, stop-recruiting discipline.

**Setup:** Choose a Scenario where you expect to build a lead. When you identify the closing window, write down your two-sentence close plan.

**Score criteria:**
- Did you correctly identify the closing window (not too early, not too late)? (1–3)
- Did you stop recruiting and commit fully to closing? (1–3)
- Did you execute the close plan within two turns of declaring it? (1–3)

## Interpreting your score

**36–45: Strong foundation.** Your fundamentals are solid. You are ready for Q2, where synergy, engine building, and advanced strategy demand a reliable base. Focus on the lowest-scoring scenario for refinement.

**25–35: Competent with gaps.** You have the concepts but some skills are not yet automatic. Identify the two lowest-scoring scenarios and spend a week practicing each before moving to Q2.

**Below 25: Replay the fundamentals.** The skills are not yet reliable under pressure. Reread the Q1 posts that correspond to your weakest scenarios and play focused practice sessions before retaking the trial.

## The honest assessment

The hardest part of the trial is scoring yourself honestly. A 2 feels generous, a 1 feels harsh. Resist the temptation to round up. A score of 30 with honest assessment is more valuable than a score of 40 with generous self-grading. The benchmark exists to show you where to invest practice time, and inflated scores hide the information you need.
