The "no surprises" philosophy: prepare for everything within your control so that the only surprises come from the game itself. Tournament day should be about execution, not logistics. Every hour spent worrying about format rules, timing, or preparation gaps is an hour not spent playing your best.

## The checklist

### Strategy preparation

- [ ] Three archetypes practiced and ready — [aggro, engine, control, or hybrid](/posts/week-35-archetypes/)
- [ ] Strategy sentence memorized for each archetype
- [ ] Resource curve sketch committed to memory for each archetype
- [ ] [Matchup plan](/posts/week-27-matchups/) framework practiced — can write three bullets within sixty seconds of seeing a Scenario
- [ ] [Flex-slot](/posts/week-28-flex-slots/) options identified for each archetype
- [ ] [Plan B](/posts/week-43-plan-b/) cards selected for each archetype
- [ ] Five or more practice games completed with each archetype in the last two weeks

### Execution preparation

- [ ] [Five-step turn sequence](/posts/week-05-turn-sequencing/) is automatic
- [ ] [Threat assessment ladder](/posts/week-07-threat-assessment/) is automatic
- [ ] [Keep/buy/skip](/posts/week-06-hand-evaluation/) evaluation is instant
- [ ] [60-second decision rule](/posts/week-33-play-faster/) practiced under time pressure
- [ ] [Closing window](/posts/week-10-closing/) recognition practiced in recent games

### Mental preparation

- [ ] [Between-round reset](/posts/week-37-mental-game/) ritual defined (physical, mental, strategic)
- [ ] Tilt triggers identified — know your personal warning signs
- [ ] Pre-tournament intention set: "I will play the game in front of me"
- [ ] Sleep, nutrition, and hydration planned for tournament day (basic but often neglected)

### Logistics

- [ ] Tournament format understood — number of rounds, Scenario pool, scoring system
- [ ] Schedule confirmed — start time, round duration, break schedule
- [ ] Equipment ready — everything you need to play is prepared and accessible
- [ ] Communication plan for co-op — pre-agreed shorthand with teammates

### Contingency planning

- [ ] What if my main archetype is countered in round one? → Play secondary, do not panic
- [ ] What if I lose the first two rounds? → Focus on execution, not standings. One round at a time
- [ ] What if I tilt? → Execute the reset ritual. Do not skip it
- [ ] What if the format is different than expected? → Adapt. The skills transfer across formats

## The night-before rule

The night before a tournament is not for cramming. Your preparation is either done or it is not. Last-minute practice sessions under pressure produce anxiety, not improvement.

The night before: review your checklist once. Confirm everything is checked. Set your alarm. Go to sleep.

## Tournament morning

Thirty minutes before the tournament starts:

1. Review your three strategy sentences (two minutes)
2. Review your matchup plan framework (two minutes)
3. Set your mental intention (one minute)
4. Stop preparing. Start competing.

## Try this

Run through this checklist one week before your next tournament. Every unchecked item is a preparation task for the coming week. By tournament day, every box should be checked.
