An engine deck is a slow investment that produces overwhelming returns. While aggressive decks start dealing damage immediately, an engine deck spends its early turns assembling components — draw cards, synergy pieces, economy generators — that individually do little but collectively produce turns so powerful that they close the game in two or three massive swings.

The risk is obvious: if the game ends before your engine activates, you lose. The reward is equally obvious: if it activates, almost nothing can stop it.

## Phase 1: Assembly

The assembly phase covers the first third of the game. Your goal is to acquire the pieces your engine needs without dying in the process.

**Identify the fuel.** Every engine needs fuel — the resource that powers it. In most deckbuilders, that fuel is card draw. Cards that draw additional cards let you see more of your deck each turn, which means your synergy pieces appear together more often. Draw is the universal engine fuel.

**Identify the output.** The engine converts fuel into output. Output might be massive attack, overwhelming recruit for a single decisive purchase, or a chain of effects that clears the board. Know what your engine produces before you start building it.

**Recruit fuel first.** During assembly, prioritize draw and filtering effects over payload cards. A draw-two card acquired on turn two generates value for the rest of the game. A finisher acquired on turn two sits dead in your deck until you have the draw to find it consistently.

## Phase 2: Protection

An engine under construction is vulnerable. Your deck is full of draw effects and setup pieces but short on attack and defense. The Scenario does not wait for you to finish building.

**Buy time, not power.** During protection, your job is survival. Recruit just enough attack to handle immediate threats. Do not over-invest in defense — every defensive card dilutes your engine. Hit the minimum threshold to keep the board from collapsing and no more.

**Lean on teammates.** In co-op, this is where role assignment pays off. The [Stabilizer and Finisher](/posts/week-12-coop-roles/) handle threats while you build. Communicate clearly: "I need two more turns before my engine is online."

**Protect the draw chain.** If a Scenario effect threatens your card draw — discarding from hand, reducing hand size, or deck disruption — that effect is a Tier 1 threat for you. Your engine cannot activate without its fuel.

## Phase 3: Explosion

The engine comes online when your draw chains produce enough extra cards that your turn generates dramatically more value than a normal turn. This is the explosion.

**Recognize the activation turn.** You will know it when it happens: you play a draw card, which draws into another draw card, which draws into a synergy piece, which triggers a bonus, and suddenly you are holding twice the cards you started with. That turn — and every turn after it — is where the investment pays back.

**Commit fully.** Once the engine activates, stop building and start converting. Every turn from now on should produce maximum output. Do not recruit more engine pieces — the engine is done. Channel everything into damage, threat removal, or whatever your output axis demands.

**Close fast.** Engine decks are strongest in the mid-to-late game but can stall if the Scenario escalates past what even a powered-up engine can handle. Once your engine is running, apply the [closing principles](/posts/week-10-closing/) aggressively. The window of engine dominance is wide but not infinite.

## Engine vs burst: when each is correct

Not every game rewards engines. Choose engine when:
- The Scenario starts slowly and escalates gradually
- The recruitment pool has strong draw and filtering effects
- Your teammates can provide early-game stability

Choose burst when:
- The Scenario is aggressive from turn one
- Draw effects are scarce in the recruitment pool
- The game rewards immediate aggression over investment

## Try this

Build an engine deck intentionally in your next game. Before the game, identify your fuel card (the draw effect you will prioritize) and your output card (the finisher that converts drawn cards into damage). Track which turn the engine activates and how many extra cards it produces per turn after activation.
