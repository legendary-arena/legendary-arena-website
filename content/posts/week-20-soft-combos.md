A soft combo is two cards that produce extra value when played together but do not require each other to function. Each card is useful on its own. Together, they are better. This is different from a hard combo where neither card does much without the other — soft combos reward you for building your deck with natural pairings rather than fragile dependencies.

## Why soft combos matter

The math of deckbuilding works against hard combos. In a thirty-card deck drawing five per turn, the probability of having two specific cards in the same hand is low — roughly ten to fifteen percent in any given turn. A strategy that depends on two specific cards appearing together fails most turns and dominates occasionally. That inconsistency loses more games than the occasional explosive turn wins.

Soft combos invert this problem. Because each card in a soft combo pair is independently useful, they contribute value every time they appear. When they appear together, you get a bonus. The baseline is good; the ceiling is great. This is the structure that produces consistently strong decks.

## Common soft combo patterns

### Draw + payload

A card that draws extra cards paired with a card that gets stronger based on cards played or hand size. The draw effect is useful on its own (more options). The payload card is useful on its own (damage or recruit). Together, the draw feeds the payload for an amplified turn.

### Economy + expensive finisher

An economy card paired with a high-cost card that requires a big purchase. The economy card generates value every turn. The finisher is the target it enables. When both are working in the same turn, the economy effect unlocks a purchase you could not otherwise afford.

### Thinning + density

A thinning card paired with any high-quality card already in your deck. Thinning removes a weak card, which increases the probability that future draws hit the strong card. The thin is useful immediately; the density benefit compounds every turn afterward.

### Trigger + payoff

A card that triggers a condition (playing a certain number of cards, having a keyword, meeting a cost threshold) paired with a card that rewards that condition. The trigger card often does something useful on its own (draw, attack, recruit). The payoff card rewards the trigger for bonus value.

## Building a deck around soft combos

You do not need to identify combos before the game starts. Soft combos emerge from smart recruitment:

1. **Recruit cards that are good independently.** This is the foundation. Never buy a card solely because it combos with something you already have. Buy it because it is good, and the combo is a bonus.

2. **Notice natural pairings.** After a few recruits, look at your deck and ask: which of these cards produce extra value when they appear together? Those pairs are your soft combos.

3. **Increase combo density.** Once you have identified a pairing, recruit additional cards that fit either side of the combo. A second draw effect pairs with the same payload card. A second thinning card accelerates the same density benefit. More entry points into the combo mean higher frequency.

4. **Do not chase the fourth copy.** Redundancy is good; excess is dilution. Three to four cards that serve as combo partners is optimal. Beyond that, you are building a hard combo deck under the guise of soft combos.

## Soft combos vs hard combos

Soft combos are your foundation. Hard combos — which we will cover [next week](/posts/week-21-setup-turns/) — are a deliberate gamble. Hard combos offer higher ceilings but lower floors. A deck built primarily on soft combos with one hard combo as a finishing move gives you the consistency to survive and the ceiling to win.

Do not build a deck of all hard combos. Do not ignore hard combos entirely. Layer them on top of a soft combo base.

## Try this

In your next game, after your third recruit, identify one soft combo pair in your deck — two cards that are each good alone but better together. Track how many turns they appear together versus apart. Note the difference in your turn quality. That gap is the bonus value of soft combo awareness.
