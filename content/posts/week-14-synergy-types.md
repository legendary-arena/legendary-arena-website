Synergy is the word every deckbuilder uses and few define precisely. It means cards working together to produce more value than they would individually. But not all synergy is created equal. There are five distinct types, and in most games, only two of them reliably determine outcomes.

## The five types

### 1. Keyword synergy

Cards that share a keyword and grant bonuses when played together. A card that says "for each other card with keyword X, gain +1 attack" creates keyword synergy. The more keyword-X cards in your hand, the more each one is worth.

**Strength:** Scales with density. A deck with eight keyword-X cards hits the bonus consistently.
**Weakness:** Requires critical mass. Two keyword-X cards in a forty-card deck rarely appear together.

### 2. Faction synergy

Cards belonging to the same group or team that unlock bonuses when combined. Similar to keyword synergy but tied to identity rather than mechanics.

**Strength:** Often comes with built-in redundancy — multiple cards in the same faction tend to reinforce each other.
**Weakness:** Faction-locked decks are predictable and vulnerable to counter-strategies that disrupt that specific faction.

### 3. Trait synergy

Cards that share a secondary attribute (cost range, card type, color) and interact through that attribute. Less obvious than keyword or faction, trait synergy often appears in effects like "draw a card for each card costing 3 or less you played this turn."

**Strength:** Can be layered on top of other synergy types.
**Weakness:** Fragile. Trait-based bonuses often require very specific conditions.

### 4. Cost synergy

Cards that interact based on recruit cost — effects that reduce costs, refund costs, or trigger when you recruit at certain price points. A card that says "when you recruit a card costing 5 or more, draw a card" creates cost synergy.

**Strength:** Powerful in economy-heavy decks that make large purchases.
**Weakness:** Narrow. Cost synergy only fires during recruitment, not during combat or threat management.

### 5. Play-pattern synergy

Cards that work together because of how you play them, not because of any shared keyword, faction, or trait. A draw card synergizes with a card that gets stronger with more cards in hand. A thinning card synergizes with every remaining card by increasing draw density. Play-pattern synergy is structural — it emerges from deck architecture rather than card labels.

**Strength:** Universally available. You do not need specific factions or keywords. Any deck can build play-pattern synergy through smart construction.
**Weakness:** Less visible. New players miss it because there is no label or icon announcing the connection.

## Why keyword and play-pattern dominate

In most games, keyword synergy and play-pattern synergy produce the most consistent results:

**Keyword synergy** rewards density. When you commit to a keyword, you recruit heavily from a narrow pool. This naturally creates a focused, lean deck — which is already good practice from your [fundamentals work](/posts/week-01-deck-checklist/). The synergy bonus is a reward for the deck discipline you should be practicing anyway.

**Play-pattern synergy** rewards architecture. Draw-into-power, thin-then-draw, economy-into-finisher — these patterns work regardless of faction or keyword because they leverage the physics of the deckbuilding format itself. A player who understands play-pattern synergy can build powerful decks from any card pool.

Faction and trait synergy are real but conditional — they depend on specific cards being available. Cost synergy is niche. Keyword and play-pattern synergy are the two you should think about first in any game.

## Identifying your deck's synergy type

After your next game, label your deck's primary synergy. What is the structural reason your cards work together? If you cannot identify one, your deck may be [the Buffet trap](/posts/week-11-beginner-traps/) — individually strong cards with no connective tissue.

## Try this

In your next game, before your first recruit, examine the available cards and identify which synergy type the recruitment pool supports best. Build toward that type intentionally. After the game, evaluate whether your synergy plan held and how much bonus value it generated.
