If you want your name on the Hall of Legends and you want it this week, this is the gauntlet to pick.

Most gauntlets are eight or nine legs. The S.H.I.E.L.D. set has **four schemes**, which makes the Hydra High Council gauntlet the shortest complete championship on the board — and completeness is the only thing standing between you and a ranked entry. There is no partial credit, but there is also not much to finish.

## Why four legs matters more than it sounds

A gauntlet entry requires a winning score on **every** leg. That completeness rule is what makes the format meaningful, and it is also what stops most people — not difficulty, attrition. Nine legs means nine separate evenings where nothing can go wrong permanently.

Four legs changes the arithmetic. It is a realistic single-sitting project at solo, or one session with a regular group. If you have been looking at the leaderboard thinking "someday," this is the one where someday is a Saturday.

The other quiet advantage: **best-per-leg scoring means retries only ever help you.** A bad run at Hail Hydra costs you nothing but time — your best score stands. With four legs, the cost of experimenting is low enough that you can afford to learn the set properly rather than playing it safe.

## What the Hydra High Council asks

The S.H.I.E.L.D. set is built around infiltration and betrayal as themes, and the schemes lean that way — *Secret Empire of Betrayal* and *Hail Hydra* both trade on the idea that your own side is compromised.

The practical read across all four legs: **do not over-commit to a single line of play.** Schemes in this set tend to punish the player who has arranged everything perfectly and has no answer when the arrangement is disrupted. A build with two ways to generate its resources will ride out a bad twist that a single-engine build will not.

Because the gauntlet leaves villain groups open, you also have a real lever here that people forget to pull: you choose what you fight. Villain-group selection is part of your run, not part of the fixture.

## The Fixed-Pool division

Every board carries two divisions. **Open** is the normal one — any heroes, leg by leg. **Fixed-Pool Championship** is the prestige version: across all four legs, the union of every hero you used must fit inside `heroCount + 2`.

| Players | Heroes per match | Fixed-Pool budget |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | 5 |
| 2–4 | 5 | 7 |
| 5 | 6 | 8 |

Four legs is also the friendliest place to attempt Fixed-Pool, and it's worth understanding why the budget is generous here rather than assuming it's tight. At solo you get five heroes across four schemes — three per match, so two substitutions total. At two-to-four players, seven heroes across four schemes is genuinely comfortable.

Which makes this the natural first Fixed-Pool attempt. The constraint is real enough to be interesting and loose enough to be survivable, unlike the nine-leg sets where a single mis-chosen core hero follows you for the whole championship.

## Heroes

Worth stating plainly, because it is the most common wrong assumption about the format: **your heroes are not restricted to the S.H.I.E.L.D. set.** The gauntlet pins the mastermind and the four schemes. Heroes come from anywhere in your collection.

The set's own twelve are thematically apt and a reasonable place to start — Agent Phil Coulson, Deathlok, Mockingbird, Quake, Dum Dum Dugan, Grant Ward, G.W. Bridge, Leo Fitz and Jemma Simmons, Melinda May, Sharon Carter, Victoria Hand, and Yo-Yo Rodriguez — but nothing stops a mixed roster, and in Open division there is no reason not to bring your best.

Suggested approach:

- **Run the legs in the order above.** S.H.I.E.L.D. vs. HYDRA War is the most straightforward introduction to the set's pressure; Secret Empire of Betrayal is the one most likely to catch out a fragile build, so meet it once you know what you're doing.
- **In Open, counter-pick freely.** There is no reason to handicap yourself; the division exists for exactly this.
- **In Fixed-Pool, choose for floor rather than ceiling.** With only four legs your core is exposed less often, but it's still the same core every time.
