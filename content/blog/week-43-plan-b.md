A Plan B is not a hybrid deck. A hybrid splits resources between two strategies. A Plan B is an insurance policy — two cards that provide a fallback win condition at minimum cost, so your primary strategy gets nearly full investment while you still have an escape hatch if it fails.

## Why two cards

One card is a slot filler, not a plan. Three or more cards is a hybrid. Two cards is the sweet spot: enough to provide a genuine alternative path, few enough to avoid meaningful dilution. In a twenty-five-card deck, two cards represent eight percent of your deck — a small price for a backup win condition.

## Designing the two-card Plan B

Your Plan B needs two components:

**Card 1: The enabler.** A card that creates the conditions for your fallback to work. This card should be useful in your primary strategy as well — it is not dead weight when Plan A is working. An economy card that doubles as fuel for Plan B. A draw card that also triggers your fallback condition.

**Card 2: The payoff.** A card that delivers the alternative win condition. This card might sit quietly in your deck while Plan A operates, but when Plan A fails and the enabler activates, the payoff card provides the output you need.

The best Plan B pairs have an enabler that is independently useful and a payoff that is acceptable (not dead) in your primary strategy.

## Trigger conditions for switching

You do not activate Plan B on a whim. You activate it when Plan A has been structurally disrupted:

- Your key synergy cards were denied or are not available
- The Scenario specifically counters your primary output type
- Your primary engine was disrupted and cannot be rebuilt in time

The trigger should be clear and recognizable. "If my primary attack source is countered, switch to economy-based VP scoring" is a clear trigger. "If things go badly, try something different" is not.

## Examples of two-card Plan B patterns

**Economy failsafe.** Primary strategy: aggro damage. Plan B enabler: a strong economy card (useful in aggro for reaching key buys). Plan B payoff: a card that converts excess economy into a non-combat win condition. If aggro is countered, pivot to economy dominance.

**Utility pivot.** Primary strategy: engine scaling. Plan B enabler: a board-control card (useful in engine for protecting your assembly). Plan B payoff: a card that rewards controlling the board over multiple turns. If your engine cannot assemble, pivot to board dominance.

**Late-game insurance.** Primary strategy: mid-range consistency. Plan B enabler: a late-game draw effect (useful in mid-range for maintaining hand quality). Plan B payoff: a high-cost finisher that closes the game in one explosive turn. If consistency is not closing fast enough, pivot to a single explosive finish.

## Plan B discipline

Two rules for Plan B management:

**Do not activate early.** Plan B is insurance, not an option. If Plan A is working, ignore Plan B entirely. Using the Plan B payoff card as part of Plan A is fine (it is in your deck anyway), but do not redirect your strategy toward Plan B unless Plan A is genuinely compromised.

**Do not invest more.** Once your two Plan B cards are in your deck, stop. Do not add more cards to "strengthen the backup." That path leads to hybrid territory and dilutes your primary strategy. Two cards is the budget. Stay within it.

## Try this

Before your next game, choose two cards as your Plan B. State the trigger condition: "I will switch to Plan B if [specific condition]." After the game, evaluate: was Plan B needed? If it was, did it provide a viable fallback? If it was not, did the two cards contribute to Plan A without causing dilution?
