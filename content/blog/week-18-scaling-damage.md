There are two ways to increase your deck's damage output. You can add more cards that deal damage — stacking flat bonuses one on top of another. Or you can add cards that multiply the damage you already have. Both increase your numbers. Only one of them scales.

## Adders vs multipliers

An adder is a card that contributes a fixed amount: "+2 attack." Every adder you add to your deck increases your potential output by a constant amount. Two adders give you four extra attack. Three adders give you six. Linear growth.

A multiplier is a card that increases the value of other cards: "double the attack of the next card you play" or "gain +1 attack for each other card played this turn." Multipliers grow with the number of cards they interact with. One multiplier with four other cards might produce eight extra attack. With six other cards, twelve. Exponential growth.

In the early game, adders and multipliers perform similarly — you do not have enough cards for multipliers to outpace flat bonuses. But in the mid and late game, multipliers pull ahead dramatically because your hand size and card quality both increase as your deck matures.

## Identifying multiplier effects

Multiplier effects come in several forms:

**Per-card bonuses.** "Gain +1 attack for each other Hero you played this turn." This scales with the number of cards in your hand and with draw effects that increase hand size.

**Conditional doublers.** "If you have played a card with keyword X, double your attack this turn." Binary — either it fires or it does not — but when it fires, the impact is massive.

**Chain triggers.** "When you play this card, you may play an additional card from your hand." Chain triggers let you play more cards, which means more synergy triggers, which means more total output. The multiplier is not on the card itself but on the turn structure it enables.

**Percentage scaling.** "Gain attack equal to your total recruit this turn." This converts one resource into another, effectively multiplying your total output by leveraging a resource you were generating anyway.

## Building around a scaling axis

A scaling axis is the dimension along which your deck grows. Choose one:

**Draw-based scaling.** Your multipliers reward having more cards in hand or playing more cards per turn. Pair these with heavy draw effects. The more you draw, the more your multipliers produce.

**Keyword-density scaling.** Your multipliers reward having many cards of the same keyword. Pair these with focused recruitment — buy deep into one keyword, not wide across several.

**Economy-to-damage conversion.** Your multipliers convert recruit points into attack or other output. Pair these with economy cards so your engine produces resources that your multipliers then convert into damage.

Pick one axis and build around it. A deck with two scaling axes usually reaches critical mass on neither.

## When adders are correct

Multipliers are not always superior. Adders are better when:

- Your deck is small and your hand size is limited. Multipliers need volume to outperform flat bonuses.
- The game is short. Multipliers need time to accumulate their advantage. In a fast game, reliable flat damage closes faster.
- Multiplier effects are not available in the recruitment pool. You cannot build around what does not exist.

Recognize the game you are in. Not every game supports a scaling strategy, and forcing one where the cards do not support it produces a deck that is neither fast nor powerful.

## Try this

In your next game, identify one multiplier effect available in the recruitment row. Build your deck to maximize that effect — recruit the cards it scales with, prioritize draw to see them together, and track your peak damage turn. Compare it to your average turn. The gap between peak and average is the power of scaling.
