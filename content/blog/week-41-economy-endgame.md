Economy is the foundation of every deckbuilding strategy. Without recruit generation, you cannot acquire the cards that win the game. But economy has a ceiling, and most players hit that ceiling without recognizing it. They keep recruiting economy cards past the point of diminishing returns, building a money machine that generates resources it cannot spend fast enough — while the Scenario escalates past what their underpowered attack can handle.

## The diminishing returns curve

Each economy card you add to your deck increases your recruit generation by a fixed amount. But the value of each additional recruit point depends on what you can buy with it.

**Early game:** Economy cards are premium. Every extra recruit point lets you acquire stronger cards sooner. The first economy card pays for itself many times over.

**Mid-game:** Economy cards are good. You have enough recruit to buy mid-range cards consistently. Additional economy lets you reach high-cost finishers.

**Late game:** Economy cards become dead weight. You have already acquired your key cards. Extra recruit points have nothing worthwhile to buy. Every economy card in your hand is a card slot that could have been attack, draw, or a synergy trigger.

The crossover point is the moment where an additional economy card adds less value to your deck than an additional payload card. Most players miss this point because economy cards feel safe — generating resources always seems useful, even when those resources have nowhere to go.

## Recognizing the crossover

Three signs you have passed the crossover:

**You end turns with unspent recruit.** If your recruit generation regularly exceeds the cost of the best available card in the row, you have more economy than you need.

**Your best buys are already in your deck.** The recruitment row no longer offers cards worth buying at your current power level. Additional recruit points are surplus.

**Your attack is lagging.** Your recruit per turn is high but your damage per turn is low. You are generating resources but not converting them into game progress.

## What replaces economy

Once you recognize the crossover, redirect your recruitment priorities:

**Attack and payload.** Cards that deal damage, clear Villains, and advance the win condition. Your economy funded the acquisition — now the acquisitions need to produce output.

**Draw and filtering.** If your economy cards are diluting your draws, prioritize draw effects that help you see your attack cards more often. Draw replaces the value that economy used to provide (access to good cards) through a different mechanism (seeing the good cards you already have).

**Thinning.** Remove economy cards that are past their useful life. A two-cost economy card that was essential on turn two is dead weight on turn twelve. Thin it to improve draw quality.

## The transition

The transition from economy focus to payload focus should be deliberate:

1. **Identify the crossover point.** Ask yourself each turn: am I generating more recruit than I can usefully spend?
2. **Shift recruitment.** Stop buying economy cards. Start buying attack, draw, and synergy pieces.
3. **Thin excess economy.** If thinning is available, remove the weakest economy cards from your deck.
4. **Accept the transition cost.** For a few turns, your deck will feel awkward — still full of economy but now recruiting payload. This is temporary. The payload cards will appear in future draws and your balance will correct.

## Try this

In your next game, define your econ cutoff before the game starts: "I will recruit no more than X economy cards." When you hit X, switch to payload regardless of what economy options appear in the row. After the game, evaluate whether X was the right number — too high, too low, or just right.
