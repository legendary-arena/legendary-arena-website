You have the lead. Your engine is running, your deck is lean, your damage output is high. You are winning. And then, somehow, you do not win. The Scheme advances. A Villain escapes. The board flips. The lead evaporates because you kept building instead of finishing.

Closing is its own skill. The mid-game rewards patience, investment, and setup. The endgame rewards aggression, commitment, and the discipline to stop recruiting.

## The closing window

Every game has a window where the leading player can convert advantage into a win. Before that window, you do not have the resources to close. After that window, the Scenario's escalation catches up and your lead shrinks. The window is not announced — you have to recognize it.

Signs the window is open:

- Your damage output per turn exceeds the threshold needed to make meaningful progress toward the win condition
- The Scenario has escalated to the point where further delay increases risk faster than your deck improves
- You have recruited all the key cards you need — additional recruits add marginal value at best

When you see these signs, shift from building to closing.

## Stop recruiting

This is the hardest part. Your engine generates recruit points every turn. The recruitment row still has appealing cards. Your brain says "that card would make my deck even better." But it would not — it would make your deck bigger. At this stage, bigger is slower, and slower is losing.

Once the closing window opens, every recruit point spent on a new card is a turn wasted. That card enters your deck but may never appear in your hand before the game ends. You paid for future value in a game that has no future. Spend those recruit points on thinning instead, or accept them as waste.

## The close plan

Before your turn begins, state your close plan in two sentences:

1. **What do I need to accomplish to end this game?** (Example: "Defeat the Mastermind's last two attacks.")
2. **How many turns will that take at my current output?** (Example: "Two turns at six attack per turn.")

Now your only job is executing that plan. Every card you play, every decision you make, serves those two sentences. If a card does not contribute to the close plan, it is noise.

## Common closing mistakes

### Continuing to build

Already covered, but worth repeating: recruiting during the closing window is the number one way to lose a won game. Your deck is finished. Use it.

### Splitting focus

When closing, you cannot fight secondary threats and close simultaneously. If a Villain appears that is dangerous but not lethal, assess whether ignoring it for one turn lets you close the game. Often the answer is yes. Taking a hit to close next turn is better than diverting damage and extending the game by three turns.

### Being conservative

The mid-game rewards caution. The endgame punishes it. If you need six attack to close and your hand has five plus a risky draw effect that might give you one more, take the risk. The downside of whiffing is a one-turn delay. The downside of playing safe is giving the Scenario another turn to escalate.

### Forgetting teammates

In co-op, closing is a team event. Communicate your close plan. If you need six damage and you have four, your partner might have two. Coordinating a joint close turn is faster than waiting for your deck to solo it.

## The "two-turn rule"

When the closing window opens, give yourself two turns. If you cannot close in two turns, reassess: is the window actually open, or did you misread it? A genuine closing window should be executable in one to two turns. If it takes longer, you either do not have enough power (keep building) or you are splitting focus (commit fully).

## Try this

Next game, explicitly announce to yourself (or your team) the moment the closing window opens. State the close plan in two sentences. Then track whether you actually stopped recruiting. If you bought a card after declaring the window open, note whether it ever appeared in your hand before the game ended.
