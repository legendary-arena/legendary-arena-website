Solo deckbuilding is a puzzle. Cooperative deckbuilding is a team sport. And team sports require roles. When every player builds independently — optimizing their own deck without considering what the team needs — you end up with three economy engines and no one who can fight the Mastermind.

Role assignment does not mean rigid specialization. It means every player knows their primary contribution so the team covers all necessary functions without duplication or gaps.

## The four roles

### Builder

The Builder focuses on economy and card acquisition. Their deck generates high recruit output, secures key cards from the recruitment row, and reaches expensive power cards faster than anyone else. The Builder is the team's engine — they invest early so the team has access to premium tools mid-game.

**Priority recruits:** Economy cards, draw effects, cost-reducers.
**Mid-game contribution:** Acquires finishers, distributes high-value recruits if mechanics allow card-sharing.
**Risk:** Slow to contribute attack. If the game demands early aggression, the Builder is a liability until their engine pays off.

### Cleaner

The Cleaner handles deck thinning, board maintenance, and utility. They remove weak cards from their own deck (and the team's, if mechanics support it), defeat low-priority Villains to keep the board manageable, and provide support effects that amplify the team.

**Priority recruits:** Thinning effects, utility cards, card-filtering.
**Mid-game contribution:** A lean, consistent deck that reliably provides exactly what is needed each turn. The Cleaner is never explosive, but never dead either.
**Risk:** Low individual power ceiling. The Cleaner enables the team but cannot solo a crisis.

### Finisher

The Finisher builds for maximum damage output. Their deck scales toward the highest-impact attacks, targeting the Mastermind and the hardest threats on the board. The Finisher accepts a slower start in exchange for a devastating mid-to-late game.

**Priority recruits:** High-attack cards, damage multipliers, synergy pieces that scale.
**Mid-game contribution:** Solves the problems no one else can. Defeats the Mastermind's attacks, clears boss-tier Villains, and delivers the closing strikes.
**Risk:** Dependent on the Builder and Cleaner to keep the game stable until the Finisher's engine activates. If the team collapses early, the Finisher's investment is wasted.

### Stabilizer

The Stabilizer manages the Scenario's threat output. They focus on preventing escalation: defeating Villains that would trigger cascading penalties, blocking Scheme advances, and absorbing damage that would overwhelm the team.

**Priority recruits:** Defensive cards, Scheme-interaction effects, broad-target attack.
**Mid-game contribution:** Keeps the game from spiraling. The Stabilizer buys time for the Builder to invest and the Finisher to scale.
**Risk:** Reactive by nature. If the Scenario does not pressure early, the Stabilizer's investment in defense is partially wasted.

## Assigning roles

Role assignment happens before the first turn. Look at three factors:

**The Scenario.** A fast, aggressive Scenario needs a Stabilizer and possibly an early Finisher. A slow, scaling Scenario gives the Builder time to invest. Match roles to the threat profile.

**Starting hands.** If one player draws a strong economy opener, they are a natural Builder. If another draws attack-heavy, they lean Finisher. Do not fight the opening hand — use it.

**Player count.** With two players, each takes a primary and secondary role. With three or four, one role per player with the fourth player (Stabilizer) serving as the flex. Not every game needs all four roles — adapt to the Scenario.

## Communication protocol

Role assignment is useless without ongoing communication. Three moments require check-ins:

1. **Start of game:** Declare roles. "I am Builder. You are Finisher." Five seconds, prevents the entire session from going sideways.
2. **Mid-game shift:** If the Scenario changes the threat profile, roles may need to adjust. The Stabilizer might signal that threats are under control and shift to support the Finisher. The Builder might announce they have acquired the key cards and are switching to Cleaner.
3. **Closing window:** When the team enters closing range, the Finisher takes priority. All other roles shift to support: the Builder provides resources, the Cleaner removes obstacles, the Stabilizer prevents last-minute disruption.

## When roles flex

Rigid role adherence is as dangerous as no roles at all. If the Finisher draws a hand with zero attack but five recruit, they should recruit — not sit on a dead hand out of principle. Roles define your priority, not your prison.

The test: if your role's primary action is available this turn, take it. If it is not, contribute whatever you can from your secondary role. The team does not benefit from a Finisher who skips three turns "waiting for attack cards."

## Try this

At the start of your next co-op game, spend thirty seconds assigning roles. After the game, assess: did the role assignment hold? Where did it need to flex? Would a different assignment have been better? This post-game review builds the intuition to assign faster in future sessions.
