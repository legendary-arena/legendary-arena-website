The deck clinic format: community members submit their builds, and we analyze three representative examples. For each deck, we identify what is working, what is not, and the single change that would improve it most. The goal is not to rebuild from scratch — it is to show that one targeted adjustment often transforms a struggling deck into a competitive one.

## Deck 1: The Collector

**The build:** Thirty-eight cards. Every faction represented. Strong individual cards across the board — nothing weak, nothing focused.

**What is working:** Card quality is high. Every card in this deck is individually solid. The player has good taste — they are selecting strong cards from the row.

**What is not working:** [Dilution](/blog/week-24-dilution/) is killing this deck. At thirty-eight cards, the best cards appear once every seven-plus turns instead of every five. The deck has no [synergy type](/blog/week-14-synergy-types/) — it is the [Buffet trap](/blog/week-11-beginner-traps/). Strong cards that do not work together produce mediocre hands.

**The one change:** Cut eight cards and commit to one faction or one keyword. The remaining thirty cards will draw more consistently, fire synergy bonuses they are currently missing, and see the best cards more often. The eight cards being cut are all individually good — but the deck does not need them.

## Deck 2: The Glass Cannon

**The build:** Twenty-four cards. Heavy attack focus. Maximum damage per turn potential is massive. Draw effects are sparse. Economy is minimal.

**What is working:** Focus. This deck knows exactly what it wants to do: deal damage. The strategy sentence is clear, and every card serves it. The lean size means high card density — the best cards appear frequently.

**What is not working:** No engine. The deck has attack cards but no draw to chain them together. On a good draw, it is devastating. On a bad draw — and in a twenty-four-card deck with no draw effects, bad draws happen frequently — it produces three or four attack instead of twelve. The [consistency](/blog/week-23-consistency/) is terrible despite the lean build because there is no mechanism to smooth variance.

**The one change:** Replace two attack cards with two draw effects. The total maximum damage goes down slightly, but the average damage goes up significantly because draw effects find the attack cards that are already in the deck. The player is not losing attack power — they are making existing attack power more accessible.

## Deck 3: The Over-Builder

**The build:** Thirty cards. Strong economy core. Excellent draw effects. Multiple synergy pieces that chain beautifully. Zero finishers.

**What is working:** The engine is beautiful. Economy generates resources, draw effects find the engine pieces, and the synergies fire consistently. This is a textbook [engine build](/blog/week-15-engine-building/) through the assembly and protection phases.

**What is not working:** The engine has no output. All fuel, no payload. The player invested so heavily in building a smooth machine that they never recruited the cards that convert the machine's output into game progress. They can generate twelve recruit per turn — and have nothing to buy with it.

**The one change:** Replace two economy cards (the deck has more than enough) with two attack-oriented finishers. The engine does not need more fuel — it needs a destination. Two finishers give the engine something to power, and the strong draw effects will find them consistently.

## Common patterns across all three

Each deck represents a different failure mode, but all three share a root cause: imbalanced investment.

- The Collector over-invested in breadth at the expense of focus.
- The Glass Cannon over-invested in output at the expense of engine.
- The Over-Builder over-invested in engine at the expense of output.

The fix in every case was the same: identify the imbalance and redirect a small number of card slots to correct it. Not a full rebuild — a two-to-three-card adjustment that rebalances the deck's architecture.

## Try this

Submit your own deck for analysis — or better, apply the clinic format to your own build. What is working? What is not? What is the one change that would improve it most? Be honest with the diagnosis.
