Casual play rewards having one good deck. Tournament play rewards having three. Tournaments present diverse Scenarios, and a player who can only execute one strategy will eventually face a Scenario that counters it. Three practiced archetypes give you coverage — the ability to match your approach to the challenge in front of you.

## Why three

One archetype gives you depth but no flexibility. Five archetypes give you flexibility but no depth — you cannot master five strategies simultaneously in a two-week prep window. Three is the practical balance: enough coverage to handle most Scenario types, few enough to practice each one until execution is smooth.

## Choosing your three

Select archetypes that cover different axes:

**Speed axis.** One fast deck (early aggression, quick closing) and one slow deck (engine building, late-game scaling). The third can be mid-range.

**Strategy axis.** One deck focused on damage output, one on board control, and one on economy-to-power conversion. Each answers a different type of Scenario.

**Comfort axis.** Include your strongest archetype — the one you play best under pressure. Your other two can be less comfortable but should cover the gaps your main archetype leaves.

A good three-deck roster: one aggressive deck that closes fast against passive Scenarios, one engine deck that scales against slow-building Scenarios, and one control deck that stabilizes against aggressive Scenarios.

## The two-week training schedule

### Week 1: Isolation drills (Days 1–7)

Practice each archetype in isolation. Play two to three games with each, focusing on execution rather than winning.

**Days 1–2:** Archetype A (your main). Focus on refining your opening sequence and closing pattern. You know this deck — sharpen it.

**Days 3–4:** Archetype B (your secondary). Focus on the elements that feel least natural. If this is an engine deck and you usually play aggro, practice the patience required during assembly phase.

**Days 5–6:** Archetype C (your tertiary). Focus on situational decision-making. When do you play this deck? What Scenarios call for it? Practice the Scenario-reading that leads you to select it.

**Day 7:** Rest. Review notes from the week. Identify one weakness per archetype.

### Week 2: Integration drills (Days 8–14)

Practice selecting between archetypes based on Scenario conditions.

**Days 8–10:** Play three-game sets where you choose your archetype after seeing the Scenario setup. Practice the decision: "This Scenario is fast, so I play Archetype A." "This Scenario scales, so I play Archetype B." The selection skill is as important as the execution.

**Days 11–12:** Play under simulated tournament conditions — time pressure, consecutive games, no breaks between Scenarios. This tests endurance and decision-making under fatigue.

**Day 13:** Final review. Can you execute each archetype without referencing notes? Can you select the right archetype within sixty seconds of seeing a Scenario? If not, spend this day on the gap.

**Day 14:** Light practice or rest. Do not overwork the day before a tournament. Trust your preparation.

## The pre-tournament checklist

Before you register:

- Can you state each archetype's strategy sentence from memory?
- Can you sketch each archetype's ideal resource curve without looking?
- Can you name the Scenario types each archetype handles and the ones it struggles against?
- Have you played at least five games with each archetype in the last two weeks?

If any answer is no, you have your prep assignment.

## Try this

Choose your three archetypes this week. Play one game with each and take notes on what felt smooth and what felt rough. Use those notes to structure your training for the coming weeks.
