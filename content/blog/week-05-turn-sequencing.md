Most deckbuilding mistakes do not happen during recruitment. They happen during the turn itself, in the order you play your cards. A hand with ten points of potential becomes seven because you committed an attack before playing the card that would have drawn you two more. Sequencing is the skill that separates a player who builds good decks from a player who actually wins with them.

## Why order matters

Every card you play changes the state of your hand. A card that draws two more cards increases your options. A card that generates recruit points increases your purchasing power. A card that deals damage reduces the threats on the board. The question is not whether to play all three — it is which one you play first.

Playing damage before draw means you attack with partial information. You might commit three attack to a Villain when the next two draws would have given you five — enough to defeat the Villain above it instead. Playing recruit effects before draw means you decide what to buy before seeing your full hand. You might recruit a mid-cost card when two more draws would have given you enough for the finisher you actually need.

The principle is simple: maximize information before making commitments.

## The 5-step turn

### Step 1 — Play all draw and card-manipulation effects

Before you do anything else, play every card that draws additional cards, filters your deck, or reveals information. This gives you the largest possible hand and the most complete picture of what your turn can accomplish.

Do not stop to evaluate partway through. Play all draw effects, then assess.

### Step 2 — Evaluate your full hand

Now you see everything available to you this turn. Count your total recruit points. Count your total attack. Identify any special abilities or conditional triggers. This is the moment to plan — not before.

Ask two questions: What is the best thing this hand can do? And: Is there a combination of plays that unlocks something the individual cards cannot?

### Step 3 — Activate synergy and trigger effects

Play cards with conditional abilities that depend on other cards being in play. Faction bonuses, keyword triggers, paired effects — these generate value only if their conditions are met. Sequence them after your draw step so you know which conditions are actually satisfiable this turn.

### Step 4 — Commit attacks

Now that you know your full attack power and have activated all bonuses, decide where to direct damage. Prioritize using the [Danger Ladder](/blog/week-07-threat-assessment/) (covered next week): immediate threats first, scaling threats second, passive threats last.

Do not split damage across multiple targets unless you can defeat both. Overkilling one Villain is better than half-killing two.

### Step 5 — Recruit and clean up

Spend remaining recruit points. Buy cards that serve your [strategy sentence](/blog/week-01-deck-checklist/). Thin your deck if thinning options are available. Then discard, draw your new hand, and end.

## The most common sequencing mistake

The single most frequent error is playing an attack card before finishing your draw step. It feels productive — you see a Villain, you have damage, you want to hit it. But committing early locks in your attack total before you know what your full hand contains.

Imagine this: you have three attack showing and a draw-two card. If you attack first, you deal three to a Villain that needs five. Wasted. If you draw first, those two cards might add two more attack — now you defeat it. Same hand, different order, completely different outcome.

The fix is mechanical: move all draw cards to the left of your hand, play them first, always.

## Practice pattern: the left-to-right sweep

Physically sort your hand each turn. Draw effects go left. Synergy triggers go center. Attack and recruit go right. Then play left to right. This removes the temptation to jump ahead. After a few sessions it becomes automatic, and your turns get both faster and more accurate.

## When to break the sequence

The 5-step order is a default, not a law. Two situations justify breaking it:

**Time pressure.** In competitive play with a clock, you may need to commit attacks before resolving every draw if the clock is about to run out. Know the sequence well enough to identify when shortcutting costs you little.

**Forced sequencing.** Some card effects require you to act before drawing (an effect that says "attack, then draw based on the result"). In those cases, the card's text overrides the default. But when you have a choice, default to information first.

## Try this

Next game, physically sort every hand before playing. Draw effects left, synergy center, attack and recruit right. Play left to right for the entire session. After the game, note how many times the draw step changed your plan for the turn. That number is the value of proper sequencing.
