The first three turns of a deckbuilding game feel low-stakes. Your hand is weak, the threats are manageable, and the recruitment row looks like a buffet of possibilities. But these turns are the highest-leverage moments in the entire game. The cards you recruit in turns one through three define the engine you will play with for the rest of the session. Get them right and your deck compounds into something powerful. Get them wrong and you spend the next ten turns trying to fix a foundation that was never solid.

## The compound interest effect

Think of early recruitment like compound interest. A card recruited on turn one enters your deck and starts appearing in your draws almost immediately. Over a fifteen-turn game, that card might appear in your hand six or seven times. A card recruited on turn eight appears maybe three times. Same card, same cost, half the value — purely because of timing.

This is why early-game decisions matter disproportionately. A modest economy card recruited on turn one generates more total value across the game than a flashy finisher recruited on turn eight.

## Two opening philosophies

### Economy-first

The economy-first opener prioritizes recruit-generating cards in the first two to three turns. You accept that your early attack will be low and that threats may stack up temporarily. The payoff comes in the mid-game, when your elevated economy lets you recruit premium cards that other players cannot yet afford.

Economy-first is correct when:
- The Scenario starts slowly and does not punish passive play early
- High-cost cards in the recruitment row offer strong scaling
- You are playing a long game where compound value matters

### Aggression-first

The aggression-first opener prioritizes attack cards to start defeating Villains immediately. You sacrifice early economy growth for board presence, clearing threats before they escalate and banking the rewards from defeated Villains early.

Aggression-first is correct when:
- The Scenario escalates quickly and punishes slow starts
- Villain defeat rewards (bonus recruit, card draw, etc.) are strong enough to substitute for dedicated economy
- The recruitment row does not offer strong economy options early

## How to choose

Read the board before making your first recruit. Three questions:

1. **What does the Scenario punish?** If it punishes passivity (escalating threats, accumulating penalties), lean aggressive. If it punishes recklessness (counterattacks, traps on engagement), lean economy.
2. **What is available to buy?** If the recruitment row has a strong two-cost economy card, economy-first is easy to execute. If it has a strong three-cost attacker with a defeat bonus, aggression-first might generate its own economy.
3. **What are your teammates doing?** In co-op, if one player goes economy and another goes aggression, the team covers both bases. Do not duplicate your partner's opening — complement it.

## The first three buys

Whatever philosophy you choose, your first three recruits should follow a pattern:

**Buy 1: Foundation.** Your first purchase is your most important. Choose the card that best enables your opening philosophy. Economy-first: the best recruit-per-cost card available. Aggression-first: the best attack card with an upside (defeat bonus, draw trigger, etc.).

**Buy 2: Reinforcement.** Your second purchase reinforces the first. If you bought economy, buy more economy or a draw card that helps you see your economy faster. If you bought attack, buy another attacker or utility that supports aggression.

**Buy 3: Pivot check.** Before your third recruit, reassess. Has the board changed? Did a teammate take the role you planned? Is a Tier 1 threat emerging that changes the priority? Your third buy is your last chance to adjust the opening before your deck's identity solidifies.

## Common opening mistakes

**Buying a finisher on turn one.** A six-cost card is dead weight until your economy can support it. Even if you can afford it with a lucky first hand, it dilutes your early draws and delays your engine.

**Spreading across too many roles.** One economy card, one attack card, one utility card in your first three buys gives you a deck that does everything poorly. Commit to a direction for at least two of your first three purchases.

**Ignoring the Scenario.** The Scenario is telling you what kind of game this will be. A fast Scenario demands a fast response. Do not play a slow economy opening against an aggressive Scenario because you read somewhere that economy-first is "always correct." Context overrides default.

## Try this

Before your next game, look at the Scenario and the starting recruitment row. Write down your first three planned recruits and your reasoning before drawing your opening hand. After the game, review: did those three buys set you up for success, or did you need to course-correct?
