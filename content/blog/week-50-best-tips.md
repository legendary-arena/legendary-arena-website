Fifty weeks of strategy distilled into twelve tips — one from each month. These are the insights that produced the largest improvements for the most players. Each one links back to the full post if you want the deep dive.

## 1. Write a strategy sentence before you build

Your deck needs a one-sentence purpose. "Recruit heavy hitters early and overwhelm the Mastermind before the Scheme escalates." Every card either serves that sentence or stays out. — [Week 1](/blog/week-01-deck-checklist/)

## 2. Balance your resource curve across three bands

Low (0–2), mid (3–5), high (6+). A balanced curve means playable hands every turn, not occasionally. — [Week 2](/blog/week-02-resource-curve/)

## 3. Draw effects first, commitments last

The five-step turn: play draw, evaluate, trigger synergies, commit attacks, then recruit. This order alone eliminates the most common source of wasted turns. — [Week 5](/blog/week-05-turn-sequencing/)

## 4. Skip more cards than you buy

The keep/buy/skip framework. Most cards in the row should be skips. Buying a "pretty good" card dilutes your deck. The discipline of skipping is the discipline of winning. — [Week 6](/blog/week-06-hand-evaluation/)

## 5. Card draw is the real damage

A card that draws two more cards does not just give you options — it compounds. Prioritize draw effects in recruitment. A deck that sees more of itself wins more games. — [Week 16](/blog/week-16-card-draw/)

## 6. Multipliers beat adders

Linear damage stacks slowly. Multiplicative damage scales explosively. Build around a scaling axis and your late-game ceiling dramatically increases. — [Week 18](/blog/week-18-scaling-damage/)

## 7. Consistency beats power

A deck that reliably produces seven damage per turn beats a deck that swings between two and twelve. Raise your floor before chasing your ceiling. Redundancy, thinning, and filtering are the tools. — [Week 23](/blog/week-23-consistency/)

## 8. Dilution is the silent deck killer

Every card you add makes every other card harder to draw. A lean twenty-five-card deck outperforms a bloated thirty-five-card deck of equally good cards. Know when to stop recruiting. — [Week 24](/blog/week-24-dilution/)

## 9. Read the matchup before you build

Before your first recruit, assess what the Scenario rewards and punishes. Write a three-bullet matchup plan. Sixty seconds of reading prevents fifteen turns of misalignment. — [Week 27](/blog/week-27-matchups/)

## 10. Find the one decision that mattered

Post-game review is not replaying the whole game — it is finding the single decision that most changed the outcome. Evaluate it. Write one rule for next time. Your personal playbook grows from here. — [Week 36](/blog/week-36-post-game-review/)

## 11. Reset between rounds

The three-step ritual: physical (stand, breathe), mental (one positive from last round), strategic (read the next Scenario). Two minutes that prevent tilt from carrying across games. — [Week 37](/blog/week-37-mental-game/)

## 12. Stop building when the closing window opens

The most common way to lose a won game: recruiting another card when you should be finishing. The engine is done. Use it. — [Week 10](/blog/week-10-closing/)

## The challenge

Pick one tip from this list — the one you apply least consistently — and master it over the next month. One tip, fully internalized, changes more games than twelve tips vaguely remembered.
