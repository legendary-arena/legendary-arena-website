Ten rules. Each one is concise, actionable, and tested under tournament pressure. Pick one to enforce this week. Add another next week. By tournament day, they should be automatic.

## Rule 1: Read the Scenario before you recruit

Every tournament round starts with a Scenario reveal. Read it completely before your first purchase. Players who start recruiting before understanding the Scenario are building for a game they have not assessed yet. Sixty seconds of reading prevents fifteen turns of misalignment.

## Rule 2: Have your first three buys planned by turn one

Your [early-game priorities](/blog/week-08-early-game/) should be decided before your first hand is drawn. Scenario → strategy sentence → first three buys. This sequence should take under ninety seconds. If it takes longer, your preparation was insufficient.

## Rule 3: Keep a lean deck

Twenty-five to thirty cards is almost always better than thirty-five to forty. Every card added past thirty should have a specific justification. "It was available" is not a justification. [Dilution](/blog/week-24-dilution/) kills more tournament decks than bad luck.

## Rule 4: Follow your sequencing framework

[Draw before commit, information before action](/blog/week-05-turn-sequencing/). The five-step turn applies under pressure exactly as it does in practice. Deviating from the framework under stress is how mistakes happen. Trust the system.

## Rule 5: Use the 60-second rule for decisions

If you cannot decide within [sixty seconds](/blog/week-33-play-faster/), take the safe option. Extended deliberation rarely improves the decision, and the time cost compounds across a multi-round tournament.

## Rule 6: Do not panic after a bad round

A loss in round one does not mean a loss in the tournament. Most tournament formats allow for one or two bad rounds. The danger is not the loss itself — it is the [tilt](/blog/week-37-mental-game/) that carries into the next round. Reset between rounds. Every Scenario is a fresh start.

## Rule 7: Play the board, not the standings

Do not change your strategy based on where you think you are in the standings. Play each round to win that round. Trying to calculate standings mid-tournament leads to suboptimal decisions driven by anxiety rather than analysis.

## Rule 8: Communicate in co-op (even under pressure)

Tournament pressure makes players go silent. They focus on their own cards and forget that [co-op roles](/blog/week-12-coop-roles/) require coordination. Five seconds of communication per turn prevents cascading failures: "I see Tier 1 at position X. Can you cover Tier 2?"

## Rule 9: Close when the window opens

Do not wait for a "perfect" closing turn. The [closing window](/blog/week-10-closing/) may be open for only one or two turns. When your output is sufficient to close and the conditions are met, commit. Every turn you delay is a turn the Scenario can escalate and close the window.

## Rule 10: Review between rounds, not during

Mid-game is for execution. Between rounds is for review. Apply the [one-decision method](/blog/week-36-post-game-review/) during the gap — find the one decision, evaluate it, write your rule. Then reset and prepare for the next round. Do not carry analysis into gameplay.

## How to use these rules

Do not try to memorize all ten for your next tournament. Instead:

- This week: pick the one rule you violate most often. Enforce it in every game.
- Next week: add a second rule.
- In two weeks: add a third.

By tournament day, three to five of these rules should be automatic. The rest will follow as you gain experience.

## Try this

Pick one rule from this list — the one that addresses your biggest competitive weakness. Enforce it for your next three games. After each game, evaluate: did following the rule change your play? Was it hard to maintain under pressure?
