A setup turn is a turn where you deliberately underperform now to overperform later. Instead of spending all your resources on immediate value, you arrange your deck, your board, or your position so that the next turn — or the turn after — produces dramatically more than two normal turns combined.

Setup turns are how experienced players generate explosive plays. They are also how inexperienced players waste turns on plans that never pay off. The difference is knowing when setup is worth the investment.

## The setup calculus

A setup turn costs you one turn of normal output. In exchange, it should produce at least two turns of output on the payoff turn. If the payoff is less than double, you would have been better off playing normally both turns.

Before committing to a setup turn, answer three questions:

1. **What is the payoff?** Be specific. "A big turn" is not a payoff — "twelve attack instead of my usual five" is a payoff.
2. **What needs to happen for the payoff to fire?** Identify the conditions. Do specific cards need to be in your hand? Does a board state need to persist? Does a teammate need to set something up?
3. **What is the probability the conditions will be met?** If the payoff depends on drawing one specific card from a thirty-card deck, the probability is low and the setup is a gamble. If the payoff depends on conditions that are already nearly satisfied, the setup is an investment.

## Reading the game state for setup opportunities

Setup turns require time — you need at least one turn where underperformance will not lose you the game. Check:

**Is the board stable?** If [Tier 1 threats](/blog/week-07-threat-assessment/) are active, you cannot afford a setup turn. Handle the emergency first.

**Is your engine online?** Setup turns work best when your deck is functional and the payoff turn will have the draw and synergy to deliver. Setting up before your engine activates means the payoff turn might not have the cards to capitalize.

**Will the game last long enough?** If the game will end in two turns, a setup turn on turn one means your payoff turn is also your last turn. The risk is that the payoff whiffs and you lose a game you would have survived by playing normally.

## Types of hard combos

Hard combos — unlike [soft combos](/blog/week-20-soft-combos/) — require specific conditions and setup:

**Two-piece combos.** Two specific cards that produce a dramatic effect together. Neither does much alone. You need both in hand simultaneously, which means either drawing heavily or using a setup turn to arrange them.

**Setup-payoff chains.** A card played this turn that creates a condition (a marker, a persistent effect, a deck arrangement) and a card played next turn that capitalizes on that condition. The setup card is useless without the payoff card following it.

**Board-state combos.** A combination that requires a specific board position — particular threats, recruitment row contents, or teammate setups. These are the hardest to arrange because you control only part of the required state.

## Risk management for setup turns

The biggest risk of a setup turn is whiffing: you invest the turn and the payoff does not materialize. To reduce this risk:

- **Have a fallback.** If the payoff does not fire, what is your Plan B? A setup turn with no fallback is a gamble. A setup turn where the setup card is still moderately useful on its own is an investment.
- **Layer soft combos underneath.** Your deck should not depend on the hard combo. If the hard combo fires, it is spectacular. If it does not, your soft combos carry the turn at a respectable level.
- **Limit setup investment.** One setup turn is an investment. Two consecutive setup turns is usually too much. The longer you spend setting up, the more likely the game state changes and invalidates your plan.

## Try this

In your next game, identify one moment where a setup turn could produce a double-value payoff. Before committing, run through the three-question calculus: what is the payoff, what are the conditions, and what is the probability? If the answer is favorable, execute the setup and track whether the payoff delivered.
