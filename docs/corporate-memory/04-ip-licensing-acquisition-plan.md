## 0. TL;DR

- **This needs TWO licenses, not one.** An official digital *Marvel Legendary*
  requires (a) **Upper Deck** for the *Legendary* system + brand, and (b)
  **Marvel/Disney** for **digital** character rights. These are separate grants —
  see §1–§2. *(This corrects an earlier draft that treated it as one entangled
  deal through Upper Deck; that was wrong — see §2.)*
- **Marvel's digital rights were never Upper Deck's to pass along.** UD's Marvel
  *Legendary* license is physical/print; digital/interactive was carved out. The
  proof: **Legendary DXP** — UD's own digital Legendary — is a **fantasy reskin**
  (the world of Alamyth), *not* Marvel, precisely because UD lacked digital Marvel
  rights. See §3.
- **There is no official digital *Marvel* Legendary anywhere — that's a genuine
  market gap, not a crowded space.** DXP (fantasy) does not compete on the Marvel
  axis. The pitch is "grow the pie" (bring Marvel Legendary to digital for the
  first time), not "compete with DXP." See §3.
- **Realistic economics:** entertainment/character royalties run **15–30%** with a
  **non-refundable minimum guarantee** + advance — and with *two* licensors you may
  stack *two* royalty cuts. A ~$10k *advance* is plausible; the total commitment is
  materially larger. Disney/Marvel grants go to well-capitalized, established
  partners; timelines run **6–18 months**. See §4.
- **Recommended path:** **Upper Deck first as the warm entry point and potential
  broker/partner**, ideally toward a **joint / tri-party deal** (Marvel + UD +
  you) — but with a distinct Marvel digital negotiation that cannot be assumed to
  come free with UD. See §5.

## 1. What Legendary Arena uses (the IP stack)

Two rights holders, two separate bundles of rights:

| Layer | Owner | What Legendary Arena uses | License needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Game system + brand** | **Upper Deck Entertainment** | The name "Legendary," the deck-building engine/mechanics (designed by Devin Low), card templates, scheme/mastermind/villain framework | Upper Deck |
| **Characters + art (digital)** | **Marvel (The Walt Disney Company)** | Character names, likenesses, logos, trademarks; the hero images at `images.legendary-arena.com` | Marvel/Disney |

The critical fact: **digital/interactive character rights are negotiated
separately from physical/print rights.** Marvel granted Upper Deck the *physical*
Legendary Marvel license; it did **not** hand UD the digital rights. So a digital
*Marvel* Legendary is not a single grant from either party — it is two grants (or
one joint deal that bundles both).

This is consistent with the existing brand posture: the business already treats
the Marvel/*Legendary* lineage as **internal-only context**, not a brand-facing
claim ([`00-business-brief.md`](00-business-brief.md) §Brand guardrails). This plan
is about making the underlying *rights* legitimate.

## 2. Who to approach — Upper Deck first, but both are required

**Correction to the earlier draft.** A prior version argued this was one deal and
Marvel was "downstream" of Upper Deck's sub-license. That was wrong: UD never held
digital Marvel rights to pass along (§3 is the proof). Both licenses are genuinely
required.

The recommended structure:

- **Upper Deck** grants the Legendary system + brand license, **and** — because UD
  has the existing physical Marvel Legendary relationship — is the natural
  **broker/partner** toward the Marvel piece.
- **Marvel/Disney** grants the digital character license. This is its own
  negotiation and cannot be assumed to arrive bundled with UD.
- **Ideal:** a **joint / tri-party arrangement** where UD is a partner who helps
  open (or co-signs) the Marvel digital deal — turning the hardest door in the
  industry (cold-approaching Disney solo) into a warm, sponsored one.

So "Upper Deck first" survives — not because Marvel is downstream, but because UD
is the **warmest entry point** and the party with the most to gain from a digital
Marvel Legendary they could never build themselves. The fallback, if UD won't
partner, is a Marvel-direct de-branded original (§6).

## 3. Legendary DXP — what it actually is, and why it matters

- **Legendary DXP is a digital adaptation of the Legendary *system* using an
  original fantasy setting (Alamyth) — not Marvel.** Developed by SkyReacher
  Entertainment, published by Upper Deck; launched August 2017 (iOS, then Android
  and Steam), shifted to fully free-to-play around mid-2018. Community resources
  map DXP names to their Marvel equivalents, confirming it is a mechanical reskin.
- **Why it's a reskin — the load-bearing fact:** Upper Deck's Marvel license did
  not cover a digital version, so they built original fantasy characters over the
  same rules. `[CONFIRM]` at the contract level, but well-supported: it is the
  reported reason DXP exists as fantasy, and it directly establishes that digital
  Marvel rights are a *separate* grant from Marvel — not something UD can license.
- **Strategic consequence — a gap, not a competitor.** There is **no official
  digital Marvel Legendary anywhere**, and UD couldn't make one. Legendary Arena
  would occupy a space the rights-holders left empty. DXP does not compete on the
  Marvel axis. This flips DXP from "risk" to "evidence of an unmet opportunity."
- **DXP's monetization is a fairness *counter*-precedent — a selling point.** DXP
  is freemium: a ~$4.99 full-game unlock, paid expansions, a premium **Gold**
  currency (energy, cosmetics, content), cosmetics, reward chests, and an **energy
  system** that gates free play. Several of those levers — energy / pay-to-skip
  timers, and randomized reward chests — **diverge from** Legendary Arena's VISION
  guardrails (NG: no energy systems, no pay-to-skip timers, no randomized/mystery
  goods). Small player base, mixed reviews (~54% positive). *"The existing digital
  Legendary gates play behind energy and sells currency; ours is fairness-first,
  zero pay-to-win"* is a cleaner brand-steward story to pitch Marvel — and it
  matches the four fairness-safe VISION streams already in place.

## 4. Realistic deal economics (replacing $10k / 10%)

Public benchmarks for entertainment/character IP — all `[CONFIRM]` with counsel
and the counterparties:

| Term | Typical range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Royalty rate** | **15–30%** of net revenue per licensor | Leaked Sony–Marvel terms imply ~19–26% on a digital title. 10% is low for Marvel-branded. |
| **Two licensors, two cuts** | Marvel *and* Upper Deck may each take a royalty | The cumulative royalty load is the real number, and it is why $10k/10% understates badly. |
| **Advance** | ~50% of first-year minimum guarantee, due ~30 days after signing | A ~$10k *advance* is plausible only if the minimum guarantee is small. |
| **Minimum guarantee (MG)** | 25–50% of projected royalties, per term/region; **non-refundable** | The MG — not the advance — is the real floor commitment (per licensor). |
| **Royalty base** | "Net revenue," carefully defined | Digital vs physical bases differ; define precisely. |
| **Term / territory** | Fixed term (often 2–5 yrs), defined territory | Renewal + termination triggers matter. |
| **Approval rights** | Licensor approves creative, marketing, use of marks | Marvel especially will want presentation approval. |
| **Reporting / audit** | Periodic royalty statements + audit rights | Requires real royalty-accounting infrastructure. |

**Reframe of the $10k:** treat it as a *possible advance line item* for one
licensor, not the cost of the whole arrangement. The genuine commitment is
`(MG + royalty rate) × up to two licensors + legal/agent costs`, and for anything
carrying the Marvel brand that package is materially larger than $10k. Budget
realistically, or scope down (e.g. a joint deal with a single blended royalty).

This informs the VISION covenant ("royalties on *all* revenue"): the royalty
routing already flagged as a launch prerequisite for the printables store
([`01-decision-log.md`](01-decision-log.md), 2026-07-04) may need to route to *two*
licensors, not one.

## 5. The approach plan

**Phase 0 — Counsel (before any outreach).** Retain an IP/licensing attorney
(entertainment/game-licensing experience). Everything below is negotiated through
or reviewed by counsel. Cheap insurance against a six/seven-figure mistake.

**Phase 1 — Diligence (facts before pitch).**
- Confirm **who holds the *Legendary* digital game rights** and whether Upper Deck
  still holds the **physical Marvel Legendary** license at all (the Topps card-
  license loss shows the relationship is in flux — if UD's Marvel physical license
  has lapsed, UD is a weaker Marvel broker and weight shifts to a direct Marvel
  approach).
- Confirm the **digital-rights boundary** in Marvel's arrangements (the §3
  `[CONFIRM]`).
- Study **Legendary DXP** as both a system precedent and a fairness contrast.

**Phase 2 — Build the pitch package.**
- **Company + product one-pager:** what Legendary Arena is, live at
  play.legendary-arena.com, the tech, the polish.
- **Traction:** real metrics (players, matches, retention, traffic). Data de-risks
  you to a brand steward.
- **Revenue model:** the four fairness-safe streams (point to VISION §Financial
  Sustainability). **Lead with the royalty-first covenant and no-pay-to-win
  guardrails** — for Marvel, "we will never sell gameplay advantage with your
  characters, and unlike the existing digital Legendary we gate nothing behind
  energy or currency" is a *selling point*.
- **Ask:** a specific, scoped proposal — ideally a **joint/tri-party structure**
  (UD system license + Marvel digital license, or one blended deal), not "please
  bless what we built."

**Phase 3 — Reach the right desks.**
- **Upper Deck** business development / licensing first — the warm entry point and
  potential Marvel broker.
- **Marvel/Disney** interactive licensing — via UD's introduction where possible,
  or a licensing agent with Disney relationships; cold-solo is the hardest path.
- Consider a **licensing agent/broker** with UD/Disney relationships (retainer or
  commission, but opens doors and normalizes terms).

**Phase 4 — NDA → term sheet(s).** Mutual NDA first, then term sheet(s) covering
the §4 terms for each licensor (or one joint sheet). Nothing signed without
counsel.

**Phase 5 — Contract, advance, compliance.**
- Execute; pay advance/MG per terms (possibly to two licensors).
- Stand up **royalty accounting + reporting** (the VISION "royalties on all
  revenue" machinery), routing to each licensor.
- Build the approval workflow for creative/marketing use of marks.

**Timeline:** realistically **6–18 months** from Phase 1 to signed deal(s); a
two-licensor / tri-party structure trends toward the longer end.

## 6. Fallback — Marvel-direct, de-branded original (one license, not two)

If Upper Deck won't partner or license, the fallback is to **drop the *Legendary*
name and its specific system** and license **only Marvel characters** for an
*original* deck-builder of your own design. That collapses the deal to a **single
Marvel license** (no Upper Deck dependency), but still requires clearing the
Marvel/Disney bar (§3–§4) and means real product rework (rename, re-theme,
re-mechanic). A strategic pivot, not a cheaper version of the same plan — but
genuinely simpler on the rights side (one licensor instead of two).

## 7. Risks to surface

1. **Present exposure (highest).** Legendary Arena today is an unlicensed
   derivative of *both* Upper Deck's *Legendary* system and Marvel's characters.
   Real and current. Approaching the rights-holders is the mitigation, but outreach
   surfaces the product to them — counsel should shape sequencing.
2. **Two-license complexity.** You need both Marvel and Upper Deck (or a joint
   deal). Two counterparties, two negotiations, potentially two royalty stacks.
3. **UD may not be able to deliver Marvel.** The digital Marvel license is a
   separate Disney negotiation; and `[CONFIRM]` UD's own Marvel relationship is in
   flux (card license lost to Topps, 2025). Do not assume UD's physical
   relationship converts to a digital Marvel grant for you.
4. **Feasibility gap.** Disney/Marvel license to well-capitalized, established
   partners. The realistic route to Marvel is *through* a partner/agent, not a cold
   solo pitch.
5. **Cost reality.** True commitment (MG + rate, per licensor, + legal/agent)
   materially exceeds $10k for anything Marvel-branded.
6. **Timeline.** 6–18 months; do not build launch/revenue plans on a fast yes.

*(Note: an earlier draft listed "DXP competition" as a risk. That was based on the
mistaken belief DXP was a Marvel product. DXP is fantasy and does not compete on
the Marvel axis — the corrected framing is the market-gap opportunity in §3.)*
