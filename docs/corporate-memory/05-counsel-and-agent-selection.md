**Scope:** how to choose the IP counsel and (optionally) licensing agent that
[Phase 0 / Phase 3 of the acquisition plan](04-ip-licensing-acquisition-plan.md)

## 1. The wall this section exists to get around: Disney takes no unsolicited pitches

Disney (including Marvel) has a **long-standing policy of not accepting unsolicited
ideas, submissions, or prototypes** — a legal shield against later "you stole my
idea" claims. Submissions are considered only when they arrive through a
**recognized channel**: a licensed agent, an entertainment attorney, or a producer
/ partner who already has a Disney relationship. (Verified — §Sources.)

**Consequence for the plan:** a cold solo approach to Marvel is not merely
low-probability, it is **policy-blocked**. That is *why* the acquisition plan's
only viable routes are (a) **Upper Deck first**, (b) an **established studio
partner** already in the ecosystem, or (c) a **warm introduction through counsel or
an agent** who has the relationship. This doc is about sourcing (c).

## 2. Selecting IP counsel (Phase 0 — do this before any outreach)

Retain an attorney with **dual** experience: major-studio/character licensing
**and** games/interactive digital rights. Pure film/TV licensing, or pure
tabletop, each covers only half the deal.

**Ask a candidate directly:**
- Have you handled **digital/interactive** character licenses (not only physical
  merchandise)? How do you treat the digital-vs-physical rights split?
- Experience with **minimum guarantees, royalty stacks, approval rights, and
  net-revenue definitions** in interactive deals?
- Any **board-game / tabletop** or **dual-rights** (system + character) matters?
- Willing to work with an **independent, pre-outreach** project — including
  advising on the public-build exposure decision (`04` Risk 7) before we approach
  anyone?
- Fee structure (hourly, flat, hourly + success)?

## 3. Licensing agents (Phase 3 — optional accelerator, not a magic key)

An agent's real value is **access** — converting a cold Disney ask into a warm one,
or opening the Upper Deck door. An agent does **not** guarantee a deal, and one
without *genuine, current* Disney/Marvel or Upper Deck relationships adds cost
without odds. Treat this as an **accelerator, evaluated as a real partnership
decision**, not a door-opener you can buy.

**Highest-weight criteria:** specific, *recent* (last ~2–3 yrs) named relationships
inside Marvel Games / Disney interactive **or** Upper Deck; genuine **digital/games**
(not only physical-merchandise) licensing experience; understanding of the **dual
system+character** license structure; transparent retainer/success-fee terms;
honest probability assessment.

**Ask a candidate directly:** which specific Disney/Marvel or Upper Deck people/teams
have you worked with in the last 24–36 months? Anonymized examples of interactive
deals you advanced? How would you sequence a deal needing both UD (system) and
Marvel (digital character) rights? Fee structure? Honest odds for a project this
size/stage? Any competing deck-building/board-game clients?

**Verified proof the agent route can work for an indie:** **Dlala Studios** (a
small UK studio) signed with a **CAA** games agent (Derek Douglas), who secured a
**Disney Interactive** deal ($250k pre-production + $3.5M) — cold indie → CAA →
Disney-games meeting. (Caveat: that specific project was cancelled when Disney
exited the games business; Disney's games posture has shifted since, so the
precedent proves the *route*, not a standing Disney appetite.) Verified — §Sources.

**Finding agents:** the standard industry sources are **Licensing International**
(formerly LIMA) member directory + events (Licensing Expo), and — often more
effective for a small team — **referrals from an entertainment attorney** or from
a digital board-game studio already shipping licensed titles. Major entertainment
licensing agencies (e.g. CAA Brand Management, Beanstalk, IMG) appear in industry
rankings, but a specific Disney/Marvel *games-digital* relationship must be
**confirmed per agency** before it means anything here — do not assume it.

---

## 4. Decision framework

**Engage** counsel/agent when: specific + recent relevant relationships;
understands the dual-license structure; transparent, success-oriented fees; gives a
realistic probability. **Pass** when: relationships are vague or dated; experience
is physical-merchandise-only; they overpromise odds/timeline; they push a large
upfront retainer with little accountability.

Sequence: **counsel first (Phase 0)**, then use an agent **selectively** — ideally
to support the **Marvel leg** *after* Upper Deck engages — rather than as the
primary path to Disney.
